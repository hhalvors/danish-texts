In 1915 a sixty-nine-year-old professor of philosophy at Copenhagen published
thirty pages in *Fysisk Tidsskrift* arguing that Einstein's special theory of
relativity contradicts the principle of non-contradiction and must therefore be
rejected. The argument is short enough to state in a paragraph, and Carl Henrik
Koch quotes its core:

> Her er altsaa to Sandheder. A har Ret; thi han har ingen Acceleration. Og B
> har ogsaa Ret; thi han har heller ingen Acceleration. Men dette gaar aabenbart
> ikke an. Der er i Logikken en Grundsætning, der siger: A er ikke Ikke-A;
> Modsigelser (i Tingenes Verden) findes ikke, og Modsigelser (i Tankens Verden)
> taales ikke. Sandheden er ikke dobbelt. Sandheden er kun én. Denne Sætning
> trænger ikke til Forsvar. Enhver Videnskab er bygget over den. Einstein's
> Teori staar derimod i Strid med den. Derfor maa den forkastes.

*So here are two truths. A is right, for he has no acceleration. And B is right
too, for he has no acceleration either. But this plainly will not do. There is a
principle in logic that says: A is not not-A; contradictions (in the world of
things) do not exist, and contradictions (in the world of thought) are not
tolerated. Truth is not double. Truth is only one. This proposition needs no
defence. Every science is built upon it. Einstein's theory, by contrast, stands
in conflict with it. Therefore it must be rejected.*

Two observers in uniform relative motion each measure the other's rod as the
shorter. Neither is accelerating, so neither can be demoted to the merely
apparent case. Kroman takes this to be a straight contradiction, and takes the
law of non-contradiction to be non-negotiable.

The objection is not eccentric — the same move was made across the international
discussion of the period — and Koch notes that Kroman's papers show he had full
command of the (after all fairly simple) mathematics of special relativity. What
makes the episode worth preserving is that in Denmark it was a *philosopher* who
opened the argument, that a physicist and a mathematician answered him in a
physics journal, and that the reply which finally settled the matter for Danish
philosophy came from Harald Høffding, in a book this collection already carries
in both languages.

The premise doing the work is not local to relativity. *Vor Naturerkjendelse*
(1883) grounds the certainty of the formal sciences in necessity of intuition —
»Al Tankenødvendighed beror til syvende og sidst paa Anskuelsesnødvendighed« —
and on that ground had already dismissed the non-Euclidean geometries as
contentless fantasies. Dansk Biografisk Leksikon names this single premise as
the source of both polemics. Kroman returned to the geometry side of it in
*Matematikken og Erkendelseslæren* (1920). The relativity articles are best read
as one application of a thirty-year-old commitment, not as a late aberration.

Kroman's own closing article confirms the shape. At printed p. 193 he writes
»Med Forfatteren af den tredie Afhandling, Hr. Dr. H. M. Hansen, er jeg
derimod erkendelsesteoretisk set meget uenig« — *with the author of the third
treatise, Dr. H. M. Hansen, I am on the other hand, epistemologically speaking,
in strong disagreement.* Three treatises answered him, and Hansen wrote the
third: items 3, 4 and 5, in that order.

Holst and Walsøe SHARE p. 13: Holst ends in its top third, and Walsøe's title
block, byline, first eight lines and two footnotes fill the rest of it, which is
why p. 14 already carries Walsøe's running head. The article was catalogued
here as pp. 14–17 until the pages were read; it is pp. 13–17.

Walsøe belongs to the debate rather than merely sitting between its parts. His
p. 17 describes itself as »denne Antydning fra Trediemand af en Mulighed for
[…] at udløse Spændingen og bilægge Striden mellem de to Parter« — *this
suggestion, from a third party, of a way to release the tension and settle the
dispute between the two parties.* He is trying to mediate between Kroman and the
physicists. Hansen's own opening at p. 18 registers the same pile-up: »Den
Interesse for Relativitetsspørgsmaalet, som flere Artikler i Fysisk Tidsskrift i
den senere Tid har givet Vidnesbyrd om…«

Hansen's 1912 lecture is the first real introduction of special relativity to a
Danish audience, and it is what provoked Kroman three years later. H. M. Hansen
is Hans Marius Hansen, Bohr's collaborator; that Bohr had also sat Kroman's
filosofikum makes the exchange a small collision of two parts of the same
university. Koch's summary of the ending is that Hansen answered the year after
the attack, that a couple of further contributions followed, and that Kroman
closed the debate without adding anything new. From about 1920 — the year
Einstein visited Copenhagen, and the year after Eddington — Danish physicists
appear simply to have accepted the theory.

Item 1 is now the only gap: Google's run of the journal skips vols 10–12, so
Hansen's 1912 lecture has to be ordered from KB. Everything else is in hand.

## Høffding's reply

*Relation som Kategori* (1921), §3d.δ »Den nye Relativitetsteori«, pp. 72–77,
answers Kroman by name — the only place in this collection where two of its
authors argue directly with each other about physics. Our transcription carries
the passage at printed pp. 75–76:

> Man har overfor den Einsteinske Hypotese […] gjort gældende, at der dog kun
> kan være én Sandhed i hvert enkelt Tilfælde, og at det vilde stride mod
> Modsigelsens Grundsætning, hvis to Iagttagere, af hvilke den ene erklærer for
> samtidigt, hvad den anden erklærer for successivt, begge skulde have Ret.

with the footnote »Kroman i Tidsskrift for Fysik, XIV, p. 15.« Høffding's answer
is that there is no reason to fear for the unity of truth even if Einstein turns
out to be right:

> Den egentlige Sandhed ligger nemlig da i den Nødvendighed, der fysisk paavises
> af, at forskellige Iagttagere under visse bestemte Betingelser faar forskellig
> Tidsopfattelse.

*The real truth lies in this case in the necessity, physically demonstrated,
that different observers under certain determinate conditions arrive at
different apprehensions of time.* An observer who is also a physicist can
explain why another observer apprehends time differently; the disagreement is
itself lawful, and the law is the truth. Høffding files this under the same
heading as Copernicus — a further instance of the lesson that the observer's own
standpoint must enter the account — and adds that if Einstein is right one does
not therefore have to philosophise in a new way. He took the point up again,
without pressing it, in *Erkendelsesteori og Livsopfattelse* (1925).

Note that Høffding gets the journal's name backwards. There is no *Tidsskrift
for Fysik*; the journal is *Fysisk Tidsskrift*. The volume and page are right —
XIV, p. 15 falls inside Kroman's pp. 1–30 — and the error is reproduced in at
least one modern bibliography, so it has had a career of its own. Anyone
searching a library catalogue on Høffding's form of the title will find nothing.

### Why the Royal Library could not find it

Two attempts to have KB digitise this failed, and the reason is in the records
rather than in the library. What KB catalogues under Kroman is not the article
but the **offprint**, and the offprints live in the **Særtrykssamlingen**, which
is filed by author and has no running call number. Both records give the
shelfmark as the bare string `Særtrykssamlingen 8°`. The 1917 record adds
`U. titelbl.` — *uden titelblad*, no title page. So the request, as it reaches
the stacks, asks someone to find an untitled fourteen-page pamphlet in an
author-filed collection with no unique locator. Add Høffding's inverted journal
name, if it travelled with the request, and failure is the expected outcome.

The general rule, which applies well beyond this item: **when a KB record says
`Særtrykssamlingen`, do not order the offprint. Order the journal volume named
in the record's »Særtr. af:« field, or find the journal elsewhere.** In this case
the journal was on Google Books all along.

KB record for the 1915 offprint: `alma99122230022205763`, 30 s., »Særtryk af:
Fysisk Tidsskrift XIV. 1. Hefte«. For the 1917 offprint:
`alma99122363662605763`, 14 s., »Særtr. af: Fysisk Tidsskrift«. The page counts
match the article page ranges exactly, 30 and 14, which is what confirms the
citations above.

## Transcribing the debate

Six of the seven items are in the Google file and the page map is now
**measured, not guessed**. `pagemap.py --detect` in each article directory
reports the offset; the three settled so far are

    Kroman 1915   printed   1-30   = PDF 331-360   (offset 330)
    Holst 1916    printed   1-13   = PDF 603-615   (offset 602)
    Kroman 1917   printed 192-205  = PDF 814-827   (offset 622)

**The numerals alone do not identify an article in this bind.** Each of vols
13, 14 and 15 has its own pp. 1-30, so the detector finds three offsets that fit
Kroman 1915's numerals perfectly -- 12, 330 and 602. What breaks the tie is
reading the pages: PDF 331 opens »Relativitetsprincipet«. / Af / K. Kroman., and
PDF 345 carries »Sandheden er ikke dobbelt«, which is printed p. 15 and so
exactly the page Høffding's footnote cites. Offset 602 turned out to be Holst's
article, which is how item 3's page map got settled in the same pass. The
detector now prints every tied candidate rather than choosing one silently.

Note also that the offset is **not constant across vol. 15**: it is 602 at the
front and 622 by p. 192, because unpaginated advertisement leaves are bound in
(PDF 795 is an ad for H. Struers kemiske Laboratorium). Each article's offset
must be measured for that article. Never carry one across.

Item 1 is the gap: Google's run of *Fysisk Tidsskrift* is vols 7–9, 13–15,
16–18, 26–27 and 30–31 — **vols 10–12 are missing**, and Danmarks Tekniske
Kulturarv has the journal listed as *ikke digitaliseret*. But unlike the
Kroman offprints, Hansen's 1912 offprint has a real call number: KB record
`alma99122809209105763`, **`8° Fys. 11882`** at Danmarks Natur- og
Lægevidenskabelige Bibliotek, »Særtr. af Fys. Tidskr. 10. 6. Hefte«. That one
can simply be ordered. (A second copy sits in the Særtrykssamlingen as
`alma99122564603005763`, S. 251–281 — do not order that one.)

Two practical notes for whoever starts. The scan is **Antiqua, not Fraktur**, so
the Fraktur model is the wrong tool and the Google text layer will be better
than usual — but still re-OCR per TRANSCRIPTION-PLAYBOOK.md §2. And this is the
first material in the collection with real **mathematics** in it: Lorentz
transformations, coordinate systems, and Kroman's own algebra. The existing
harness has never had to carry display formulas, so budget for that rather than
discovering it at batch three.

### Copyright — not all of this can be published yet

The collection's usual assumption, that anything 19th-century is long out of
copyright, does not hold here. Danish term is life plus seventy.

| Author | Died | Public domain in Denmark |
|---|---|---|
| K. Kroman | 26 July 1925 | since 1996 — **clear** |
| Helge Holst | 1944 | since 2015 — **clear** |
| H. M. Hansen | 13 June 1956 | **1 January 2027** |
| C. E. Walsøe | *not established* | **unknown — held indefinitely** |

So items 2, 3, 6 and 7 — both Kroman articles and both Holst pieces — can be
transcribed and published now. Items 1 and 5, Hansen's two contributions, are
still in copyright for a few months yet. Item 4 is unresolved: nothing is known
here about C. E. Walsøe, not even a death date, so his term cannot be computed
and his article must not be published until someone establishes it. The article
itself is a dead end: the byline is bare »Af / C. E. Walsøe.«, with no title,
affiliation, town or dateline anywhere, and the *) on its title is the
Redaktion's own disclaimer, not a note on the author. Transcribing them now is fine; publishing them is not, and
the debate can go up complete at the new year. Worth deciding deliberately,
since it is also the first time this collection would carry a 20th-century text
whose author's term has not run.

## Open questions
- **Does Kroman answer Høffding?** Høffding's reply is 1921 and Kroman lived to
  1925, but the *Fysisk Tidsskrift* debate had closed in 1917. Whether he
  responded anywhere — the 1920 *Matematikken og Erkendelseslæren* is the
  obvious place to look — is unsettled.
- **Jørgen Jørgensen in *Spectator* I (1916), pp. 384–387.** Listed in the DBL
  bibliography for Kroman and dated to the middle of the debate. Very possibly a
  sixth item in it; not yet seen.
- **Émile Meyerson cites Kroman** in *Identité et réalité* (1908) and in *De
  l'explication dans les sciences* (1921). Høffding's correspondence with
  Meyerson is already in this catalog, so there may be a line running from
  Kroman through Høffding to Meyerson worth following.
- **Koch, »Kristian Kroman«**, *Danish Yearbook of Philosophy* XIII (1976),
  pp. 147–166, is the standard scholarly treatment and has not been consulted.
  The account above leans on Koch's popular article in *Uniavisen* instead.
