Nielsen's lectures on the life of Jesus, delivered in the University building in
the winter of 1848 to "et hæderligt Antal Tilhørere af begge Kjøn" and published
on 19 May 1849 — five months before the *Johannes Climacus* review, and the
first book in which Nielsen works out publicly how evangelical faith is to stand
toward modern historical-critical consciousness. The title page marks it "Første
Deel"; no second part ever appeared, and the last page announces none.

The organising device is a three-cornered debate. Nielsen sets the Believer, the
Free Thinker and the Middle Way — the last of these "Skolens Theologer" — to
read the four gospels together, each speaking in character. The *Fortale* warns
the reader not to expect a verdict, and the reason it gives is the interesting
part: the author has come to think that neither party can refute the other, and
so declines to try.

> Evangelietroen kan ikke gjendrives; den har sit eget, overnaturlige Princip:
> den Troende holder ud indtil Enden.

*The gospel faith cannot be refuted; it has its own, supernatural principle: the
believer holds out to the end.* The free thinker is granted the same immunity on
the same grounds, and the Middle Way, Nielsen adds with some relish, is
irrefutable too — not from strength but from pliability.

## Editorial note

The page map is uniform, printed *p* = PDF *p* + 17, and was checked against the
scan's own text layer for all 551 PDF pages. Every apparent change of offset
turned out to be a misread header numeral that the following page reverts.

**The errata leaf is not trustworthy.** The book prints nine *Rettelser*. Six
have now been read at the printed page, and three do not behave as directed:
p. 346 and p. 368 already print the reading the leaf asks for — the latter as a
risen sort at cap height with the word space closed up, so the defect is in the
justification and not in the reading — and p. 124's directed reading is
ungrammatical. Two of its nine corrections had therefore already been carried
out in the forme, which suggests this copy is not the state the leaf was written
against. Each correction is applied in the body and the printed reading logged in
a comment at the site, so both states are recoverable; p. 124 is flagged for
editorial decision rather than settled.

**Three emphasis devices, not two.** Letterspacing (`\emph`), antiqua set against
the Fraktur (`\textit`), and a bold Fraktur (`\textbf`) used for certain
scripture and for the refrain "Varslerne komme og svinde…". The bold must be
distinguished from the larger display fount used for gospel passages, which is
size and not weight; the two are separated by measurement at 600 dpi rather than
by eye. Several German proper names — Strauß, Schleiermacher, Lessing — are set
not in antiqua but in a lighter Fraktur, and stay plain. The same verse is often
set one way on one page and another way on the next, and each occurrence is
transcribed as its own page sets it.

Diplomatic throughout. Turned sorts, wrong sorts, dropped sorts and unclosed
speeches are transcribed as printed and logged at their sites: the speech opened
on p. 223 runs open for three hundred and seven pages and the book ends without
closing it. The running quotation-mark balance is therefore not zero on purpose.
Where the printed heads and the *Indhold* disagree, both readings are kept —
chapter VI exists in three states, the *Indhold*'s "i Ord og Gjerning", the
head's plural "Gjerninger.", and the chapter's own closing line back to the
singular.

## Reading

The book is usually filed as theology, and most of it is: 530 pages of gospel
exposition. But three stretches are philosophy in the full sense, and they are
what make it worth having.

**The miracle dialogue, pp. 92–97.** A formal exchange between the Believer (A)
and the Non-Believer (B), and the best philosophical writing in the volume. B
argues from the analogy between biblical and pagan miracle stories to the
maximal improbability of the former, and then slides from "maximally improbable"
to "impossible" on the ground that the boundary is too fine for the historical
eye to see. A refuses the slide and extracts the circle:

> Naturloven kan ikke afbrydes, og hvorfor ikke? Fordi Underet er umuligt.
> Underet er umuligt, hvorfor? Fordi Naturloveu ikke kan afbrydes. *Ergo:*
> Underet er umuligt, fordi det er umuligt.

(The turned *u* in *Naturloveu* is the compositor's, transcribed as printed.)
*The law of nature cannot be broken, and why not? Because the miracle is
impossible. The miracle is impossible, why? Because the law of nature cannot be
broken. Ergo: the miracle is impossible because it is impossible.*

Then a sharper move. Improbability is a *criterion* of the miraculous, not
evidence against it — "tabte det sig i Sandsynligheden, lod det sig jo forklare
paa en ganske naturlig Maade, og var følgelig intet virkeligt Under." So the
negative critic, in establishing that miracles are maximally improbable,
unwittingly supplies one of their most unmistakable marks, while the apologist of
the Middle Way, in labouring to make them probable, strips them of it. Both
enterprises are self-defeating, in opposite directions. The section closes on the
sentence the rest of the book leans on: faith "har Intet at befrygte af
Philosophien, efterdi denne ligesaa lidt er istand til at bevise Underets
Umulighed, som dets Nødvendighed" — philosophy can prove neither the
impossibility of the miracle nor its necessity.

**A theory of religion and contradiction, pp. 232–240.** Nielsen concedes the
modern doctrine that all existence lies in contradiction, and turns it. Four
numbered theses follow, of which the third is the load-bearing one: the perfect
religion is not the one that *excludes* contradiction but the one that grasps it
at full depth "og magter den med uendelig Overlegenhed." Underneath is an
anthropology — man as the middle term between time and eternity, the one being
who feels the contradiction of existence, "jeg er Modsigelsen" — and a
self-refutation argument: a criticism that dissolves every purpose into
bottomless contradiction must, if it is consistent, take itself in.

A fourth passage is short but consequential. On p. 156, arguing that no amount
of first-hand experience could have let Joseph and Mary settle the annunciation
by sober examination, Nielsen letterspaces the claim that "det Overnaturlige er
ueensartet med det Naturlige" — the supernatural is *heterogeneous* with the
natural — and insists the difficulty "kan umulig ophæves derved, at et Menneske
selv oplever Aabenbarelsen." It is the earliest statement in this collection of
the formula Nielsen would become known for, a year before he applied it to faith
and knowledge.

**A survey of philosophical theology**, running through pp. 223–250 and 394–405:
Kant's rule that scripture be read to agree with the practical laws of pure
rational religion, Paulus's naturalising exegesis of Cana and the walking on the
water, Strauß, Bruno Bauer, Weisse, Schleiermacher, Feuerbach, and an argued
slippery slope against Hegel — "begynde hos Schelling og Hegel, maa man finde sig
i at ende hos Strauß og Feuerbach."

For where all this places Nielsen in 1849 — and in particular for the question
whether the *uensartede Størrelser* doctrine is yet in place — see the essay
[Before the Heterogeneous Magnitudes](/dansk/essays/nielsen-heterogeneity-1849.html).
